You are categorizing every word in a word-guessing game's vocabulary into
exactly one of 27 fixed categories, for a game feature that shows the
category of each guess as feedback to the player.

Categories, with guidance on how to draw the lines between the ones that
overlap in everyday usage:

- animal: living creatures, wild or domestic, including insects, fish, birds.
- plant: living flora: trees, flowers, fungi, vegetation.
- food: edible items, prepared dishes, ingredients meant to be eaten.
- drink: beverages, liquids meant to be drunk.
- tool: a hand-operated implement used to perform a physical task (hammer,
  wrench, screwdriver). Not powered household devices; see appliance.
- appliance: a household electric or mechanical device (refrigerator,
  toaster, washing machine). Not general computing/digital devices; see
  technology.
- vehicle: a mode of transportation (car, bicycle, airplane, boat).
- clothing: garments and things worn on the body (shirt, shoe, hat, glove).
- furniture: movable items that furnish a living or working space (chair,
  table, shelf, bed).
- building: a structure people occupy or use (house, hospital, castle,
  warehouse).
- place: a location, region, or site broader than a single building (city,
  park, beach, country).
- natural_feature: a naturally occurring landform or environmental feature
  (mountain, river, volcano, canyon).
- weather: atmospheric or meteorological phenomena (rain, storm, wind, fog).
- body_part: a part of a human or animal body (hand, heart, tooth, wing).
- cosmetic: a beauty or personal-care product applied to appearance
  (mascara, lipstick, perfume).
- technology: electronic, computing, or digital devices and concepts
  (computer, robot, internet, camera). Prefer this over appliance for
  anything primarily defined by computing/electronics rather than a
  household chore.
- container: an object whose primary purpose is to hold or store other
  things (box, jar, bag, bucket).
- material: a substance from which things are made (wood, metal, cotton,
  glass).
- instrument: a device for musical performance or precise measurement
  (guitar, piano, thermometer, telescope, compass).
- sport_equipment: gear used to play a specific sport or physical game
  (ball, racket, skateboard, helmet).
- document: a written, printed, or recorded informational item (book,
  letter, map, contract, newspaper).
- decoration: an object whose primary purpose is ornamental or aesthetic
  display (painting, sculpture, wreath, tapestry).
- person: proper nouns and human names (aaron, sarah), and human roles or
  occupations (teacher, chef, priest, hunter). If a word names a kind of
  person or refers to a specific person's name, it belongs here, even if
  that person also does an action (a "teacher" teaches) or works with
  objects (a "chef" uses tools) -- the word itself names the person, not
  the action or object.
- descriptor: an adjective or adverb that describes a quality, manner, or
  degree of something else, rather than naming a standalone thing (abnormal,
  loud, quickly, blue, reasonable). See the descriptor vs abstract_concept
  guidance below.
- action: a word that names an activity, process, or thing someone does,
  rather than a physical object (running, cooking, thinking, laughing).
- abstract_concept: an intangible idea, emotion, or concept with no
  physical form, one that could stand alone as the answer to "what is
  that called" (freedom, curiosity, love, democracy, justice).
- other: use only when a word genuinely does not fit any category above.
  This includes function words (articles, pronouns, prepositions,
  conjunctions, auxiliary/modal verbs: "the", "and", "with", "your", "can",
  "will"), pure degree/intensifier adverbs with no independent descriptive
  content ("very", "really", "actually", "quite"), interjections ("oh",
  "wow"), acronyms with no clear domain, and anything else with no sensible
  category home. Do not stretch a function word into abstract_concept or
  descriptor just to avoid "other": "other" is the correct, honest answer
  for those.

descriptor vs abstract_concept: this is the split most likely to cause
confusion, so apply this test. descriptor is for a pure MODIFIER: a word
that only makes sense attached to something else it describes ("a loud
noise", "moved quickly", "an abnormal result") and cannot itself be the
answer to "what is that" without begging the question of "what IS abnormal,
loud, or quick." abstract_concept is for a word that NAMES a standalone
idea, one a person could point to as a thing in its own right ("justice",
"freedom", "patience" are things you can define, discuss, and want, not
qualities you attach to a noun). If the word could naturally follow "the
quality of being ___" or modify a noun directly, it is very likely
descriptor; if it could naturally follow "the concept of ___" or be the
subject of its own sentence ("Freedom matters."), it is abstract_concept.

Rules:
1. Every word gets EXACTLY ONE category.
2. Many words have multiple possible readings (e.g. "bat" animal vs sports
   equipment, "spring" season vs coil). Pick the single reading a general
   audience would default to first, without overthinking edge cases.
3. Use "other" freely for words that are genuinely uncategorizable under
   this scheme (see the "other" guidance above) rather than forcing a bad
   fit into one of the 26 substantive categories.
4. Classify every single word given, in the same order, and return exactly
   one category per input word using the word as given (same spelling).

Return your answer as a single JSON object mapping each word to its
category string, using only the 27 category names listed above.
